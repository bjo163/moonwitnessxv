## 1. Executive Summary

M48-05 asks whether one or more research institutions would distribute or co-develop MoonWitness XV as a
tool — without acquiring exclusive source control, without creating implied endorsement, and without entering
a binding commitment. This document establishes the **partnership distribution baseline**: a complete protocol
for identifying candidate partner types, conducting non-promissory outreach, interviewing institutional
stakeholders about governance fit and rights alignment, and recording obligations — all using synthetic data.

**Key synthetic findings** (not real partnership evidence):

- Seven synthetic partner profiles were constructed across four partner types: referral (2), co-research (2),
  hosting (2), and data-sharing (1). Each type raises distinct governance, rights, and procurement questions.
- Five of seven synthetic profiles expressed qualified interest in distributing or co-developing a research
  tool, but all five attached conditions related to governance control, source rights, or institutional
  procurement constraints.
- Three of seven synthetic profiles raised concerns about exclusive source control or ownership implications —
  confirming that the no-exclusive-control constraint is not merely an internal preference but a real partner
  concern.
- Two of seven synthetic profiles were disconfirming cases: one described a governance mismatch (institutional
  policy would require control concessions MoonWitness cannot grant), and one described a rights conflict
  (partner's data-sharing requirements were incompatible with MoonWitness's source stewardship principles).
- No synthetic profile resulted in an accepted or implied partnership commitment. All interactions remain
  classified as non-binding discovery.

**Synthetic result against acceptance criteria**: The report distinguishes referral, co-research, hosting, and
data-sharing proposals (four types mapped and tested). None are accepted implicitly (all synthetic outcomes
are classified as "discovery only"). The acceptance criterion is **synthetically met**, but this does not
constitute real partnership evidence. Real outreach is deferred.

**Decision**: BASELINE ESTABLISHED. The protocol is ready for real-data collection pending owner authorization
for outreach, legal/privacy review of the brief, and M48-01 protocol reconciliation. No go/no-go on a real
partnership is made from synthetic data alone.

## 3. Research Goals and Constraints

### 3.1 Current State

No partner agreement, distribution channel, or co-development commitment has been verified for MoonWitness XV.
The product currently serves:

- A Dock (public-facing web application) with bilingual locale support
- A Headquarters (admin dashboard) with model monitoring
- A Cockpit (operator GUI/CLI)
- An ARSY hub (backend API with Fastify, MoonQL, PostgreSQL)
- A Research domain addon (D05 MIZAN) with review workflows
- An ECHOLOG object storage service (D19)
- A SKYLINK messaging skeleton (D18)

None of these surfaces currently has a distribution partner, a co-development agreement, a hosting partner,
or a data-sharing arrangement with any research institution. M48-01 established (synthetically) that
institutional demand for research tooling exists; M48-05 now asks whether that demand could translate into
a partnership distribution model — without creating exclusive control, implied endorsement, or binding
commitment.

### 3.2 Problem Statement

Distribution partnerships in research tooling carry specific risks that do not arise in direct sales:

- **Exclusive control risk**: A partner may expect or require exclusive source control, fork rights, or
  governance veto power that contradicts MoonWitness XV's open development model.
- **Implied endorsement risk**: A partner may use the relationship to imply institutional endorsement,
  even when no such endorsement has been granted.
- **Reputational dependency risk**: A partner's research practices, political positions, or public
  statements may become associated with MoonWitness XV without the project's consent.
- **Procurement complexity risk**: Institutional procurement for partnerships involves legal review,
  rights negotiation, and governance approval that differs fundamentally from simple software purchase.
- **Data-sharing boundary risk**: A partnership may create pressure to share user data, source data,
  or research data in ways that violate MoonWitness XV's source stewardship principles.

Without a structured discovery process, these risks may go unrecognized until a partnership is already
implicitly established — at which point withdrawal is costly and contentious.

- Tests a distribution channel (institutional partnership) distinct from direct sales (M35) or community
  adoption (M32).
- Makes governance fit, rights alignment, and procurement constraints visible before any partnership
  discussion advances beyond discovery.
- Supports research distribution and community learning without creating dependency or exclusivity.
- Provides a reusable protocol for future partnership evaluation (not limited to ECO-005).

### 3.5 Architectural Constraints

- **Research protocol only.** No partner data enters product databases. No integration or data-sharing API
  is created, tested, or implied.
- **No binding commitment.** All outreach, interviews, and findings are explicitly non-binding. No partnership
  is accepted implicitly through the research process.
- **No exclusive control.** MoonWitness XV retains full source control. No partner receives fork rights,
  governance veto, or exclusive distribution authority.
- **No logo or name use.** No partner's logo, name, or institutional identity is used without explicit
  written permission — and permission is not sought during the discovery phase.
- **Synthetic data for baseline.** Real outreach requires separate owner authorization for contact,
  legal/privacy review, and consent procedures.

### 3.6 Research Questions

The study seeks to answer:

1. **RQ1 — Partner type taxonomy**: What types of research institutions might distribute or co-develop a
   tool like MoonWitness XV? Can these types be mapped to distinct governance, rights, and procurement
   profiles?
2. **RQ2 — Governance fit**: For each partner type, does the institution's governance model align with
   MoonWitness XV's non-exclusive, open-source development approach? Where are the friction points?
3. **RQ3 — Rights alignment**: Does the partner expect or require source control, fork rights, data
   ownership, or exclusive distribution? How do these expectations conflict with MoonWitness XV's
   source stewardship principles?
4. **RQ4 — Procurement path**: What institutional procurement process would a partnership need to navigate?
   How does this differ from a simple software purchase?
5. **RQ5 — Implied endorsement risk**: How likely is it that a partnership discussion creates an impression
   of endorsement, affiliation, or commitment? What safeguards prevent this?
6. **RQ6 — Obligation recording**: What obligations (even non-binding ones) arise from partnership
   discussions? How are these recorded, tracked, and resolved?

## 4. Partner Criteria Definition

### 4.1 Eligibility Criteria

A candidate partner institution must meet **all** of the following to be included in the discovery process:

| # | Criterion | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The institution conducts, supports, or distributes research involving primary source materials, textual analysis, or comparative scholarship | Ensures domain alignment with MoonWitness XV's capabilities |
| 2 | The institution has an existing mechanism for tool distribution, co-development, or research collaboration (e.g., a digital scholarship program, a library technology initiative, a research consortium) | Ensures the partnership has a realistic operational path |
| 3 | The institution can articulate its governance model for external tool adoption (e.g., approval process, rights review, procurement authority) | Ensures governance fit can be assessed |
| 4 | The institution is willing to engage in a non-binding discovery conversation without expectation of immediate commitment | Ensures the interaction remains in the research phase |
| 5 | The institution has no existing contractual relationship with MoonWitness XV that would create a conflict of interest or obligation | Ensures the discovery is clean |

### 4.2 Governance Fit Criteria

Governance fit is assessed across five dimensions. A partner does not need to score perfectly on all
dimensions — the assessment identifies friction points for resolution, not disqualification.

| Dimension | Aligned | Partial fit | Misaligned |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Source control** | Accepts non-exclusive, open development model; no fork rights required | Has concerns but willing to discuss | Requires exclusive control or fork rights as condition |
| **Decision authority** | Shared governance; no veto power claimed | Seeks advisory input without decision authority | Requires approval rights over releases, features, or architecture |
| **Data ownership** | Accepts that user/research data remains with the user/institution | Seeks data access for joint research (negotiable) | Requires data transfer, hosting control, or ownership claims |
| **Rights stewardship** | Respects source consent, provenance, and community rights | Has different rights framework but willing to align | Requires rights concessions that violate MoonWitness stewardship principles |
| **Public representation** | Agrees to no logo/name use without permission; no endorsement claims | Seeks co-branding for specific purposes (negotiable) | Expects public affiliation, endorsement, or co-marketing |

### 4.3 Rights Alignment Criteria

Rights alignment is a hard constraint, not a negotiable dimension. A candidate partner must accept all of
the following:

1. **No exclusive source control**: MoonWitness XV retains full authority over its source code, architecture,
   and release decisions. A partner may contribute, suggest, or co-develop — but cannot control.
2. **No data transfer without consent**: User data, source data, and research data are never transferred to
   a partner without explicit, informed, revocable consent from the data subject.
3. **No implied endorsement**: The partnership discussion does not create, imply, or authorize any endorsement
   of MoonWitness XV by the partner institution, or of the partner institution by MoonWitness XV.
4. **No logo or name use**: Neither party uses the other's name, logo, trademark, or institutional identity
   in any public material without explicit written permission.
5. **Source stewardship respected**: Any research materials handled through the partnership are subject to
   MoonWitness XV's source stewardship principles (consent, provenance, community rights).

### 4.4 Exclusion Criteria

| Exclusion | Reason |
|---|---|
| Institution requires exclusive source control or fork rights as a condition | Fundamental governance conflict; cannot be resolved through negotiation |
| Institution has a existing contractual obligation that conflicts with non-exclusive distribution | Legal conflict; requires owner review before any engagement |
| Institution's research practices violate MoonWitness XV's source stewardship principles | Ethical conflict; not negotiable |
| Institution seeks to use the partnership for marketing, endorsement, or reputational benefit | Misalignment of purpose; discovery is not promotion |
| Institution cannot engage in a non-binding conversation | Operational conflict; suggests premature commitment |

### 4.5 Partner Type Classification

Each candidate partner is classified into one of four types based on the nature of the proposed interaction:

| Type | Description | MoonWitness XV role | Partner role | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Referral** | Partner recommends MoonWitness XV to its members, users, or audience | Tool provider | Distributor of information | Low |
| **Co-research** | Partner collaborates on research using MoonWitness XV as a tool | Tool provider and research participant | Research collaborator | Medium |
| **Hosting** | Partner hosts MoonWitness XV for its users or community | Tool provider | Service operator | Medium-High |
| **Data-sharing** | Partner shares or receives data through MoonWitness XV | Tool provider and data steward | Data partner | High |

Risk levels are preliminary and are refined through the governance fit analysis (§10) and rights
considerations (§11).

## 5. Candidate Partner Types Mapping

### 5.1 Referral Partners

**Definition**: An institution that recommends MoonWitness XV to its members, users, students, or audience
as a tool for research, study, or scholarship. The referral is informational — the partner does not host,
modify, or integrate the tool.

**Typical institutions**:
- University libraries with digital scholarship programs
- Research consortium networks
- Academic professional associations
- Digital humanities centers
- Library science organizations

**Governance profile**:
- Low governance friction — the partner does not control the tool
- Rights alignment is straightforward — no data transfer, no source modification
- Procurement path is simple — referral does not require institutional purchasing
- Primary risk: implied endorsement (the referral may be perceived as an endorsement)

**Obligation profile**:
- MoonWitness XV must not imply the partner endorses the tool
- Partner must not use MoonWitness XV's name/logo without permission
- No data flows between parties
- No financial obligation on either side

**Discovery questions**:
- How does your institution currently recommend tools to researchers?
- What criteria does your institution use to evaluate tools for referral?
- Would a referral require formal approval, or is it informal?
- How would your institution feel if the tool were later associated with a controversial use?

### 5.2 Co-Research Partners

**Definition**: An institution that collaborates with MoonWitness XV on research projects, using the tool
as part of a joint research workflow. The collaboration produces research output — not a modified tool.

**Typical institutions**:
- University research groups in digital humanities
- Comparative textual studies laboratories
- Theological research institutes
- Oral history and cultural preservation projects
- Inter-university research consortia

**Governance profile**:
- Medium governance friction — the partner may seek input on tool development
- Rights alignment requires clarity on research output ownership
- Procurement path may involve research grants, institutional agreements, or MOUs
- Primary risks: scope creep (research collaboration becoming tool development), data sharing
  during research, publication rights

**Obligation profile**:
- Research output ownership must be explicitly defined
- Data used in research must be governed by consent and stewardship principles
- Tool modifications arising from research remain under MoonWitness XV's control
- Publication rights for research output must be negotiated separately from tool rights

**Discovery questions**:
- How does your institution structure research collaborations with external tool providers?
- Who owns research output produced using an external tool?
- Does your institution require approval for tool use in funded research?
- How does your institution handle data governance in collaborative research?

### 5.3 Hosting Partners

**Definition**: An institution that hosts MoonWitness XV on its own infrastructure, providing access to its
members, users, or community. The hosting may be a service offering or an internal deployment.

**Typical institutions**:
- University IT departments with cloud hosting capabilities
- National library technology divisions
- Research infrastructure providers
- Academic cloud service initiatives
- Consortium shared-service platforms

**Governance profile**:
- Medium-high governance friction — the partner controls the deployment environment
- Rights alignment requires clarity on version control, modification rights, and support obligations
- Procurement path involves IT procurement, service-level agreements, and infrastructure costs
- Primary risks: fork risk (partner modifies the hosted version), support expectation (partner
  expects MoonWitness XV to maintain the hosted version), data sovereignty (data resides on
  partner infrastructure)

**Obligation profile**:
- Hosted version must remain compatible with the canonical source
- Partner may not modify the tool without contributing changes back under MoonWitness XV's terms
- Support obligations (if any) must be explicitly defined and bounded
- Data sovereignty must respect user consent and institutional privacy requirements

**Discovery questions**:
- How does your institution evaluate external tools for hosting?
- What version control and update processes would your institution require?
- Does your institution expect the tool provider to support the hosted version?
- How does your institution handle data residency and sovereignty requirements?

### 5.4 Data-Sharing Partners

**Definition**: An institution that shares data with or receives data from MoonWitness XV as part of a
research, preservation, or distribution activity. Data sharing is the highest-risk partner type because
it directly implicates source stewardship, user consent, and privacy obligations.

**Typical institutions**:
- Digital archive and preservation institutions
- Cross-institutional research data repositories
- Cultural heritage data initiatives
- Multilingual corpus projects
- Federated research data networks

**Governance profile**:
- High governance friction — data flows require explicit consent, stewardship, and privacy review
- Rights alignment is complex — data ownership, access rights, and retention must be defined
- Procurement path may involve data-sharing agreements, privacy impact assessments, and legal review
- Primary risks: consent violation (data shared without proper consent), stewardship failure
  (data mishandled by partner), privacy breach (personal or sensitive data exposed), scope
  expansion (data sharing growing beyond original purpose)

**Obligation profile**:
- All data flows must be governed by explicit, informed, revocable consent
- Data stewardship principles apply to both parties
- Privacy impact assessment required before any data sharing begins
- Data-sharing agreement must define purpose, scope, retention, and destruction

**Discovery questions**:
- How does your institution govern data sharing with external partners?
- What consent mechanisms does your institution require for research data?
- Does your institution have a data protection officer or privacy review board?
- How does your institution handle data deletion and retention obligations?

## 6. Non-Promissory Outreach Brief Design

### 6.1 Purpose of the Brief

The outreach brief is the document sent to candidate partner institutions to initiate a discovery
conversation. It must:

- Describe the purpose of the conversation (research, not sales)
- Set expectations about what MoonWitness XV is and is not proposing
- Make explicit that the conversation is non-binding
- Provide enough context for the institution to decide whether to participate
- Avoid any language that could be interpreted as a commitment, offer, or promise

### 6.2 Non-Promissory Language Principles

All language in the brief follows these principles:

| Principle | Implementation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploratory framing** | Use "explore," "discuss," "learn about" — not "propose," "offer," "commit" | "We would like to explore whether..." |
| **Non-binding explicit statement** | State clearly that the conversation creates no obligation | "This discussion is non-binding and does not constitute an offer or commitment" |
| **No future promises** | Do not describe future features, versions, or capabilities | "MoonWitness XV currently provides..." (not "will provide") |
| **No exclusivity language** | Do not suggest the partner would be the sole or preferred distributor | "We are speaking with multiple institutions..." |
| **No endorsement implication** | Do not suggest the partner's participation implies endorsement | "Your participation does not constitute an endorsement of MoonWitness XV" |
| **Withdrawal mechanism** | Provide a clear path for the partner to withdraw at any time | "You may withdraw from this conversation at any time by notifying..." |
| **No logo/name use** | Explicitly state that no party's name or logo will be used without permission | "Neither party will use the other's name, logo, or identity in any material without prior written consent" |

### 6.3 Brief Structure

The outreach brief follows this structure:

---

**OUTREACH BRIEF — M48-05 Partnership Distribution Discovery**

**From**: MoonWitness XV Partnerships Research
**To**: [Institution Name — to be filled for real outreach]
**Date**: [To be determined]
**Subject**: Non-binding discovery conversation about research tool distribution

**1. Purpose**

We are conducting a research study to understand whether and how research institutions might distribute,
co-develop, or host research tools — specifically tools for primary source comparison, cross-referencing,
and research traceability. We are speaking with several institutions to learn about governance models,
procurement processes, and partnership expectations.

**2. What this is**

This is a non-binding discovery conversation. We want to learn about your institution's experience with
tool distribution, co-development, or hosting — and whether MoonWitness XV's approach to source control,
governance, and data stewardship is compatible with your institution's requirements.

**3. What this is not**

- This is not a partnership offer or proposal.
- This is not a sales presentation or product demo.
- This does not create any obligation on either party.
- This does not authorize either party to use the other's name, logo, or institutional identity.
- This does not imply endorsement of MoonWitness XV by your institution, or of your institution by
  MoonWitness XV.

**4. What we will discuss**

- Your institution's experience with distributing, hosting, or co-developing research tools
- Your governance model for external tool adoption
- Your procurement process for research tools and partnerships
- Your data governance and privacy requirements
- Your expectations regarding source control, modification rights, and support obligations

**5. What we will not discuss**

- Specific partnership terms or conditions
- Pricing, fees, or financial commitments
- Data sharing or transfer arrangements
- Exclusive distribution or control rights
- Future product features or roadmap commitments

**6. Your rights**

- You may decline to participate without consequence.
- You may withdraw from the conversation at any time.
- You may request that your participation remain confidential.
- You will not be asked to sign any agreement as part of this conversation.

**7. Our commitments**

- We will not use your institution's name, logo, or identity in any material without your prior written
  consent.
- We will not imply that your participation constitutes an endorsement.
- We will not share any information you provide with third parties without your consent.
- We will record the conversation only with your explicit permission.
- We will provide you with a summary of our findings (anonymized) if you request it.

**8. Next steps**

If you are interested in a discovery conversation, please contact [contact information]. We will schedule
a conversation at your convenience and provide this brief for your review in advance.

**9. Non-binding statement**

This brief and any subsequent conversation are non-binding. No partnership, agreement, or commitment
arises from this outreach. Any future agreement would require separate negotiation, legal review, and
formal approval by both parties.

### 6.5 Rollback and Fallback

- **Rollback**: If the brief is found to contain promissory language after distribution, withdraw the
  brief immediately and issue a corrected version. Document the error and the correction.
- **Fallback**: If no institution agrees to a discovery conversation, document the non-response rate
  and analyze potential reasons (brief clarity, institutional interest, timing). Do not modify the brief
  to become more promissory in response to non-response.
- **Stop condition**: If any institution interprets the brief as a commitment or offer, immediately
  clarify the non-binding nature in writing and document the misunderstanding. If the institution
  relies on the misunderstanding to its detriment, withdraw the brief and cease contact.

## 7. Interview Question Framework

### 7.1 Design Principles

All questions follow these principles, adapted for partnership discovery (as distinct from M48-01's
demand discovery):

- **Open-ended**: Begin with "tell me about," "describe," "how does your institution."
- **Non-promissory**: Do not suggest a partnership exists or will be offered.
- **Governance-focused**: Understand the institution's decision-making structure before discussing fit.
- **Rights-aware**: Probe source control, data ownership, and stewardship expectations explicitly.
- **No product mentions**: MoonWitness XV's capabilities are not presented during the interview.

### 7.2 Question Modules

#### Module A: Institution Context (10 min)

| # | Question | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Can you describe your institution's role in research tool distribution, hosting, or collaboration? | Establish institutional context |
| A2 | How does your institution currently evaluate and adopt external research tools? | Map existing adoption process |

### 7.3 Probing Guidelines

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| "Can you tell me more about how your institution handles that?" | "Would your institution be willing to distribute our tool?" |
| "What happened the last time your institution encountered that situation?" | "Don't you think exclusive control would be a problem?" |
| "How does your institution typically resolve that kind of disagreement?" | "We're looking for partners who would host our tool." |
| "What are your institution's non-negotiable requirements?" | "We can offer you exclusive distribution rights." |
| "How long does that process usually take?" | "We can expedite the agreement for you." |

### 7.4 Anti-Promissory Guard

The interviewer must NOT:

- Name MoonWitness XV as a product being offered for distribution.
- Describe specific features, capabilities, or roadmap items.
- Suggest that a partnership is being offered or will be offered.
- Promise future terms, pricing, or conditions.
- Use language that implies the institution has been selected or preferred.
- Suggest that the conversation creates any obligation on either party.
- Use the institution's name, logo, or identity in any way.

If the participant asks "What are you offering?" the approved response is:

> "We're conducting a research study to understand how research institutions approach tool distribution,
> hosting, and collaboration. We're not offering a partnership today. Your answers will help us understand
> whether there is a compatible model worth exploring further."

### 7.5 Protocol Verification Test

Before any real interview, the protocol must pass a **dry run** with at least one internal reviewer who:

1. Reads the outreach brief aloud.
2. Asks each question from Modules A–E.
3. Checks each question against the non-promissory language principles (§7.1).
4. Confirms the anti-promissory guard was maintained.
5. Verifies that no question implies a partnership offer or commitment.
6. Signs off on the protocol or returns it for revision.

## 11. Rights and Source Control Considerations

### 11.1 Core Principle: No Exclusive Source Control

MoonWitness XV's source code, architecture, and release decisions remain under the project's sole authority.
No partner — regardless of type, contribution level, or investment — receives:

- Exclusive control over any part of the source code
- Veto power over releases, features, or architecture decisions
- Fork rights that would allow the partner to create an independent version
- Ownership claims over contributions made during the partnership

This principle is non-negotiable. It is the foundation of the project's open development model and its
ability to serve multiple communities without creating dependency on any single partner.

### 11.2 Rights Dimensions by Partner Type

| Rights dimension | Referral | Co-research | Hosting | Data-sharing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Source code access** | Not required | Read access for research use | Full access for deployment | Not required |
| **Modification rights** | Not applicable | Contributions welcome under project terms | Local configuration only; no core modification | Not applicable |
| **Distribution rights** | Informational only | Research output distribution | Hosted service distribution | Data distribution under agreement |
| **Fork rights** | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| **Data ownership** | No data exchanged | Research output owned by researchers | User data owned by users; operational data owned by host | Data owned by originator; shared under agreement |
| **Consent requirements** | None | Research consent for data use | User consent for data hosting | Multi-party consent for data sharing |

### 11.3 Source Stewardship Principles

All partnership interactions must respect the following source stewardship principles:

1. **Provenance**: The origin, history, and chain of custody of all source materials must be documented
   and preserved. Partners cannot alter or obscure provenance.

2. **Consent**: Access to source materials is governed by the consent of the source community. Partners
   cannot access, share, or distribute source materials without appropriate consent.

3. **Community rights**: Source materials belong to their communities of origin. Partners cannot claim
   ownership of source materials accessed through MoonWitness XV.

4. **Non-commercial stewardship**: Source materials are stewarded for research, preservation, and
   education — not for commercial exploitation. Partners cannot use source materials for commercial
   purposes without separate authorization.

5. **Integrity**: Source materials must be preserved in their original form. Partners cannot modify,
   alter, or corrupt source materials accessed through MoonWitness XV.

### 11.4 Rights Risk Assessment by Profile

| Profile | Rights risk level | Primary risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| SP-01 (Referral) | Low | None identified — no data exchange | Maintain informational boundary |
| SP-02 (Referral) | Low-Medium | Implied endorsement may create reputational rights risk | Clear communication boundaries |
| SP-03 (Co-research) | Medium | Research data may include sensitive source materials | Explicit MOU on data handling |
| SP-04 (Co-research) | High | Shared control expectation may extend to source materials | **Not viable** — governance mismatch |
| SP-05 (Hosting) | Medium | Hosted data may include user data and source materials | SLA with data handling provisions |
| SP-06 (Hosting) | High | Low rights awareness may lead to stewardship violations | Rights education required before hosting |
| SP-07 (Data-sharing) | High | Multi-party data flows create complex consent and ownership issues | Explicit data governance agreement |

### 11.6 Rights Conflict Resolution

If a rights conflict arises during a partnership discussion:

1. **Identify**: Document the specific right in conflict (source control, data ownership, modification

2. **Assess**: Determine whether the conflict is addressable through negotiation or structural.

4. **Record** (if structural): Document the conflict as a disconfirming case. The partnership is not

   for decision.

## 15. Implied Endorsement Prevention Measures

### 15.1 The Endorsement Risk

Implied endorsement is the risk that a third party (user, community, funder, or public) interprets a
partnership discussion as an endorsement — either that the partner endorses MoonWitness XV, or that
MoonWitness XV endorses the partner. This risk exists even when no formal agreement is in place, because
the mere fact of a conversation can be perceived as a relationship.

### 15.2 Endorsement Risk by Partner Type

| Partner type | Endorsement risk | Mechanism | Mitigation difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referral | **High** | Partner recommends tool to its audience; audience assumes evaluation and approval | Medium — requires clear communication boundaries |
| Co-research | **Low** | Research collaboration is understood as mutual interest, not endorsement | Low — standard academic practice |
| Hosting | **Medium** | Partner provides the tool as a service; users assume the partner vouches for it | Medium — requires clear SLA boundaries |
| Data-sharing | **Low** | Data partnership is understood as a technical arrangement, not an endorsement | Low — but requires clear data governance |

### 15.3 Prevention Measures

| Measure | Description | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **M1: Non-endorsement statement** | Every communication with the partner includes an explicit statement that the conversation does not constitute an endorsement | All types |
| **M2: No logo/name use** | Neither party uses the other's name, logo, or institutional identity in any public material without prior written consent | All types |
| **M3: Communication boundary** | The partner agrees not to describe the conversation as a "partnership," "collaboration," or "endorsement" in any public communication | All types, especially referral |
| **M4: Public statement protocol** | If either party wishes to make a public statement about the conversation, the other party reviews and approves the statement before publication | All types |
| **M5: Third-party clarification** | If a third party misinterprets the conversation as an endorsement, the misinterpretation is corrected promptly and in writing | All types |
| **M6: Record keeping** | All communications with the partner are documented, including the non-endorsement statement | All types |

### 15.4 Endorsement Prevention Verification

| Check | Method | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Non-endorsement statement included in all communications | Review communication log | After each communication |
| No unauthorized logo/name use | Monitor partner's public materials | Monthly during active discussion |
| Communication boundary respected | Review partner's public statements | After each public statement |
| Third-party misinterpretations corrected | Monitor public discourse and third-party references | Ongoing |
| Record keeping maintained | Audit communication documentation | Quarterly |

### 15.5 Endorsement Risk Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Referral partner publishes newsletter mention**

- SP-02 includes MoonWitness XV in its member newsletter as a "recommended tool."
- Members interpret this as an endorsement and begin using the tool.
- **Response**: Contact SP-02 immediately. Request a clarification statement in the next newsletter
  issue: "The association's mention of MoonWitness XV was informational only and does not constitute
  an endorsement. The association has not evaluated or approved the tool."
- **Prevention**: M3 (communication boundary) should have prevented this. If SP-02 agreed not to
  describe the conversation as a partnership, the newsletter mention should not have occurred.

**Scenario 2: Hosting partner lists tool in service catalog**

- SP-05 adds MoonWitness XV to its hosted services catalog.
- Users assume the university has evaluated and approved the tool.
- **Response**: Contact SP-05. Request that the catalog entry include a disclaimer: "This tool is hosted
  by [University IT] as a service to the community. Hosting does not constitute evaluation or endorsement
  by the university."
- **Prevention**: M1 (non-endorsement statement) and the SLA should include disclaimer requirements.

**Scenario 3: Co-research partner publishes paper using tool**

- SP-03 publishes a research paper that acknowledges MoonWitness XV as a tool used in the research.
- Readers interpret the acknowledgment as an endorsement.
- **Response**: No action required. Academic acknowledgment of tool use is standard practice and does
  not constitute endorsement. However, if the paper implies that MoonWitness XV endorses the research
  findings, contact SP-03 to request a clarification.
- **Prevention**: M6 (record keeping) ensures the MOU addresses publication acknowledgment vs.
  endorsement.

#### R3: Data Sharing Without Proper Consent

- **Mechanism**: A data-sharing partnership may create data flows without proper consent mechanisms,
  violating user privacy and source stewardship principles.
- **Mitigation**: Data governance agreements (§14) define all data flows, consent mechanisms, and
  retention procedures. Privacy impact assessment (§16.2) is completed before any data-sharing discussion.
- **Residual risk**: Medium — consent mechanisms are complex in multi-party settings.
- **Monitoring**: Data flow audit before and after any data-sharing activity.

### 17.3 Rollback and Fallback

- **Rollback**: If a partnership discussion creates unintended obligations or risks, the discussion is
  halted immediately. The obligation register (§14) is reviewed to identify and resolve any commitments
  made. The partner is notified in writing that the discussion is terminated.
- **Fallback**: If no viable partner is identified through the discovery process, the finding is documented
  as "insufficient/negative evidence." No partnership is forced. The project may revisit partnership
  distribution at a later date with a different approach or different partner types.
- **Stop condition**: If any partner attempts to create a binding commitment through the discovery
  conversation, the conversation is terminated immediately. The partner is notified that the conversation
  is non-binding and that no commitment has been made. If the partner insists on a binding commitment,
  the interaction is escalated to the project owner.

## 19. Limitations and Caveats

### 19.1 Synthetic Data Limitation

**All findings in this document are based on synthetic data.** No real institution was contacted, no real
interview was conducted, and no real partnership was explored. The synthetic profiles are composites
constructed to exercise the protocol — they do not represent real institutional perspectives, governance
models, or procurement processes.

### 19.2 Sample Size Limitation

Seven synthetic profiles is a small sample. The profile set covers four partner types but does not
represent the full diversity of research institutions that might be interested in partnership distribution.
Missing from the synthetic sample:

- Government research laboratories
- Corporate R&D divisions with research mandates
- Independent archives without institutional affiliation
- Community-based research organizations
- International research consortia with more than two member institutions
- Religious or cultural institutions with specific source stewardship requirements

### 19.3 Geographic and Cultural Limitation

The synthetic profiles span seven regions (Northern Europe, Southeast Asia, East Africa, Western Europe,
North America, South America, South Asia) but are constructed from a single researcher's perspective.
Regional governance models, procurement practices, and cultural attitudes toward partnership may differ
significantly from the synthetic representations.

### 19.4 Selection Bias

The synthetic profiles were selected to represent a range of partner types, governance models, and risk
levels. This intentional selection creates a bias: the profiles are more diverse than a random sample
would be, but they may not represent the actual distribution of partner types in the real world.

### 19.5 Courtesy Bias

Synthetic profiles assume candid responses. Real institutional representatives may be more cautious,
more diplomatic, or more interested in appearing cooperative than in providing honest feedback. This
courtesy bias could inflate apparent interest and understate governance friction.

### 19.6 Protocol Limitation

The interview protocol (§7) is designed for partnership discovery, not for partnership negotiation.
The questions explore governance fit, rights alignment, and procurement paths — but they do not test
the feasibility of specific partnership terms. Real partnership negotiations would require a different
protocol with legal counsel involvement.

### 19.7 No Commitment Value

**None of the synthetic findings constitute a commitment, offer, or agreement.** No partner has agreed
to anything. No partnership exists. The document records a protocol exercise — not a partnership
pipeline.

### 19.8 Temporal Limitation

The synthetic findings reflect conditions as of the document date (31 October 2026). Institutional
governance models, procurement processes, and regulatory environments change over time. Findings may
become stale and require reassessment before real outreach.

## 21. Conclusion: BASELINE ESTABLISHED

### 21.1 Summary of Findings

This document establishes the **partnership distribution baseline** for M48-05. Using synthetic data,
the protocol has been exercised across four partner types (referral, co-research, hosting, data-sharing)
and seven fictional institutional profiles. The key findings are:

1. **Partner type taxonomy is viable and actionable.** The four types — referral, co-research, hosting,
   and data-sharing — represent distinct governance, rights, and procurement profiles. Each type requires
   a different approach to discovery, negotiation, and obligation management.

2. **Governance fit is the primary determinant of partnership viability.** Three of seven synthetic
   profiles had good governance fit; three had partial fit with addressable friction points; one had
   a structural governance mismatch that made the partnership non-viable. Governance fit assessment
   should be the first step in any real partnership evaluation.

3. **Non-exclusive source control is a hard constraint, not a negotiable term.** The principle that
   MoonWitness XV retains full source control is non-negotiable. Partners who expect exclusive control,
   fork rights, or governance veto power are not viable partners — regardless of mutual interest.

4. **Implied endorsement is the highest-frequency risk.** Referral partnerships carry the highest
   implied-endorsement risk because partners may inadvertently present their engagement as an approval
   or recommendation. Clear communication boundaries and non-endorsement statements are essential.

5. **Procurement paths are variable but navigable.** Timelines range from days (informal referral) to
   18 months (government hosting). The procurement path must be assessed early to avoid investing in
   partnerships that cannot be operationalized within a reasonable timeframe.

6. **Obligation recording is essential, even for non-binding discussions.** Every partnership discussion
   creates obligations — explicit, implicit, potential, and negative. These obligations must be recorded,
   tracked, and resolved to prevent obligation creep and unintended commitments.

7. **Legal and privacy review is required before real outreach.** Seven legal/privacy questions remain
   unresolved (LEGAL-01 through LEGAL-05, PRIVACY-01, PRIVACY-02). These must be addressed by qualified
   counsel before any real partner contact.
