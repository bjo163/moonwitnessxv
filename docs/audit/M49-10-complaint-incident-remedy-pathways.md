# M49-10 Complaint, Incident and Remedy Pathways

**Task ID:** M49-10  
**Status:** BASELINE ESTABLISHED  
**Date:** 2026-10-31  
**Owner:** Independent safeguarding lead  
**Dependencies:** M49-03 (RACI), M49-07 (volunteer roles)

## Objective

Define safe feedback, complaint, safeguarding incident, and appeal paths before any program contact. Ensure complainants can report without public identity, urgent cases have clear escalation, and confidentiality limits/response timing are explicit.

## Scope

Governance protocol only — no public form, sensitive case database, or implementation. All scenarios are fictional tabletop exercises.

---

## 1. Report Type Taxonomy

### 1.1 Feedback (Non-Urgent)

**Definition:** General comments, suggestions, or observations about program operations that do not allege harm, policy violation, or urgent risk.

**Examples:**
- "The training materials were hard to understand."
- "I wish meetings were scheduled earlier in the day."
- "The volunteer coordinator was very helpful."
- "The reimbursement process took longer than expected."

**Characteristics:**
- No allegation of harm or misconduct
- No immediate risk to beneficiaries, volunteers, or program
- Can be addressed through normal program improvement processes
- Anonymous feedback accepted and encouraged

**Response target:** Acknowledge within 5 business days; address or explain decision within 30 days.

---

### 1.2 Complaint (Non-Urgent Policy Violation)

**Definition:** Formal allegation that program staff, volunteer, or partner violated policy, acted unprofessionally, or failed to meet program commitments, without immediate safety risk.

**Examples:**
- "The coordinator did not follow the reimbursement policy."
- "A volunteer was rude to a beneficiary."
- "The program did not provide the accommodation I requested."
- "My data was shared with someone who shouldn't have seen it (no safety risk)."

**Characteristics:**
- Allegation of policy violation or unprofessional conduct
- No immediate safety risk to any person
- Requires formal investigation and response
- Complainant identity can be kept confidential (not anonymous)
- Complainant has right to appeal decision

**Response target:** Acknowledge within 3 business days; investigate and respond within 30 days (can extend to 60 days with explanation).

---

### 1.3 Safeguarding Incident (Urgent or Serious)

**Definition:** Allegation or observation of harm, abuse, exploitation, harassment, discrimination, or serious policy violation that creates immediate or significant risk to beneficiaries, volunteers, staff, or program integrity.

**Examples:**
- "A volunteer touched a beneficiary inappropriately."
- "A staff member is making racist comments."
- "A beneficiary is being exploited by a partner organization."
- "A volunteer disclosed beneficiary identities to unauthorized persons."
- "A staff member is under the influence of alcohol during program activities."
- "A beneficiary reports sexual harassment by a program volunteer."

**Characteristics:**
- Allegation of harm, abuse, exploitation, or serious misconduct
- Immediate or significant safety risk to one or more persons
- Requires urgent investigation and intervention
- Mandatory reporting to authorities if legal threshold met
- Complainant identity protected to maximum extent allowed by law
- Complainant has right to independent advocate and appeal
- May require program suspension or personnel removal

**Response target:** Acknowledge within 24 hours; immediate safety assessment within 48 hours; investigation initiated within 72 hours; resolution target 30 days (can extend to 60 days with safeguarding lead approval and complainant notification).

---

### 1.4 Unsafe Disclosure (Crisis)

**Definition:** A beneficiary, volunteer, or staff member discloses experience of abuse, violence, trafficking, exploitation, or other serious harm that requires immediate safety intervention and potentially mandatory reporting to authorities.

**Examples:**
- "I was abused by a family member and now I'm afraid to go home."
- "I'm being trafficked and need help."
- "A beneficiary told me they are being exploited, and I don't know what to do."
- "I witnessed violence at a program site."

**Characteristics:**
- Direct or indirect disclosure of serious harm
- Immediate safety risk to discloser or others
- Requires crisis response, not investigation
- Mandatory reporting to authorities if legal threshold met (varies by jurisdiction)
- Discloser identity protected to maximum extent allowed by law
- Discloser has right to immediate support (medical, psychological, legal)
- May require emergency shelter, medical care, or law enforcement involvement

**Response target:** Immediate response (within 1 hour of disclosure); safety assessment within 2 hours; crisis intervention initiated within 4 hours; mandatory reporting decision within 24 hours (if applicable).

---

### 1.5 Retaliation Allegation

**Definition:** Allegation that a person who reported feedback, complaint, incident, or disclosure experienced adverse action (termination, demotion, harassment, exclusion, threats) as a result of reporting.

**Examples:**
- "I was fired after reporting a safeguarding incident."
- "A volunteer is being excluded from meetings after filing a complaint."
- "My supervisor is giving me bad assignments because I reported misconduct."
- "A partner threatened to withdraw funding after I reported exploitation."

**Characteristics:**
- Allegation of adverse action linked to reporting
- May constitute separate safeguarding incident
- Requires immediate investigation and protection
- Complainant has right to independent advocate
- Retaliation is itself grounds for personnel action or program suspension
- Can be reported through any channel (feedback, complaint, incident, or direct to safeguarding lead)

**Response target:** Acknowledge within 24 hours; immediate protection assessment within 48 hours; investigation initiated within 72 hours; resolution target 30 days.

## 2. Reporting Channels

### 2.1 Channel Matrix

| Channel | Feedback | Complaint | Safeguarding Incident | Unsafe Disclosure | Retaliation | Accessibility |
|---------|----------|-----------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------|---------------|
| **Online form** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Screen-reader compatible; available 24/7; multiple languages |
| **Email** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Encrypted if possible; response within 24 hours |
| **Phone** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Business hours; voicemail after hours; relay service available |
| **In-person** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | By appointment or drop-in; interpreter available |
| **Written letter** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Mailed to program address; logged within 3 business days |
| **Anonymous form** | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | No follow-up possible; no identity collected |
| **Direct to safeguarding lead** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Emergency contact available 24/7 |
| **Direct to independent advocate** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Available during business hours; confidential |
| **Third-party hotline** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | External service; anonymous option; 24/7 |

### 2.2 Channel Selection Guidance

**For feedback:** Any channel. Anonymous form encouraged if complainant uncomfortable identifying themselves.

**For complaints:** Any channel except anonymous form (complainant must be identifiable for investigation and appeal). Email or online form preferred for documentation.

**For safeguarding incidents:** Any channel. Direct to safeguarding lead or third-party hotline preferred for urgency. Phone or in-person if immediate safety risk.

**For unsafe disclosures:** Phone, in-person, or direct to safeguarding lead preferred. If discloser is in immediate danger, call emergency services first.

**For retaliation:** Any channel. Direct to safeguarding lead or independent advocate preferred if retaliation involves program staff.

### 2.3 Accessibility Requirements

All channels must provide:

1. **Language access:** Materials and interpreters available in program's working languages
2. **Disability access:** Screen-reader compatible forms; sign language interpreters; accessible meeting locations
3. **Literacy access:** Oral reporting option; plain language materials; visual aids
4. **Technology access:** Phone and in-person options for those without internet
5. **Cultural access:** Gender-matched reporter option if requested; culturally appropriate communication
6. **Timing access:** 24/7 emergency channels; flexible scheduling for non-urgent reports

## 3. Confidentiality and Identity Protection

### 3.1 Confidentiality Levels

| Level | Definition | When Applied | Who Knows Identity |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Anonymous** | No identity collected or recorded | Feedback only | No one |
| **Confidential** | Identity collected but not disclosed without consent | Complaints, incidents, retaliation | Complainant, designated investigator, safeguarding lead (on need-to-know basis) |
| **Protected** | Identity disclosed only as required by law or for safety | Unsafe disclosures, mandatory reporting | Complainant, safeguarding lead, legal counsel, authorities (if mandatory) |
| **Public** | Identity disclosed with consent or as required by program governance | None by default | Complainant consents; or program governance requires (rare) |

### 3.2 Confidentiality Limits (Must Be Explained to Complainant)

**Confidentiality is not absolute.** Limits include:

1. **Legal mandatory reporting:** If law requires reporting (e.g., child abuse, elder abuse, imminent harm), program must comply even if complainant objects.
2. **Safety necessity:** If withholding identity creates imminent safety risk to complainant or others, program may disclose on need-to-know basis.
3. **Investigation requirement:** If investigator cannot proceed without knowing identity (e.g., need to interview complainant), program may request consent to disclose to investigator.
4. **Legal process:** If court or regulatory body subpoenas records, program must comply.
5. **Program governance:** If board or funder requires incident report, program may disclose aggregate data (not individual identity) or disclose with complainant consent.

**Program must:**
- Explain these limits before complainant provides identity
- Document that limits were explained
- Obtain informed consent for confidential (not anonymous) reporting
- Minimize identity disclosure to absolute necessity
- Notify complainant if disclosure is required (unless doing so creates safety risk)

### 3.3 Identity Protection Measures

1. **Separate storage:** Complainant identity stored separately from report content
2. **Access control:** Only designated investigator and safeguarding lead can access identity
3. **Encryption:** Electronic records encrypted at rest and in transit
4. **Physical security:** Paper records in locked file; access log maintained
5. **De-identification:** Reports shared with program staff, partners, or funders use case numbers, not names
6. **Retention limits:** Identity records destroyed after retention period (see Section 6)
7. **Breach protocol:** If identity is disclosed unauthorized, notify complainant within 24 hours; investigate; mitigate

## 4. Response Roles and Responsibilities

### 4.1 Role Assignment (from M49-03 RACI)

| Role | Feedback | Complaint | Safeguarding Incident | Unsafe Disclosure | Retaliation |
|------|----------|-----------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Program Coordinator** | R (acknowledge, address) | R (acknowledge) | I (notify safeguarding lead) | I (notify safeguarding lead) | I (notify safeguarding lead) |
| **Safeguarding Lead** | I (quarterly report) | A (oversee investigation) | R/A (lead investigation) | R (crisis response) | R (lead investigation) |
| **Independent Advocate** | I (if requested) | C (advise complainant) | C (support complainant) | R (support discloser) | C (advise complainant) |
| **Program Director** | I (quarterly report) | I (appeal decision) | I (personnel action) | I (crisis decision) | I (personnel action) |
| **Legal Counsel** | I (if legal risk) | C (advise on investigation) | C (advise on mandatory reporting) | R (mandatory reporting decision) | C (advise on investigation) |
| **Board Chair** | I (annual report) | I (if program risk) | I (if program risk) | I (if program risk) | I (if program risk) |

**R** = Responsible (does the work)  
**A** = Accountable (owns the decision)  
**C** = Consulted (provides input)  
**I** = Informed (kept in the loop)

### 4.2 Designated Investigator Requirements

For complaints, safeguarding incidents, and retaliation allegations, program must designate an investigator who:

1. **Independent:** Not involved in the reported conduct; no conflict of interest with complainant or respondent
2. **Trained:** Completed safeguarding investigation training (minimum 16 hours)
3. **Experienced:** Has conducted at least 3 investigations (or co-investigated with experienced lead)
4. **Available:** Can begin investigation within 72 hours of assignment
5. **Impartial:** Committed to finding facts, not defending program or any party
6. **Confidential:** Understands and commits to confidentiality and identity protection requirements

**If no internal investigator meets these requirements:**
- Engage external investigator (contracted in advance)
- Partner with another organization to share investigator
- Board member with qualifications may serve (if no conflict)

### 4.3 Independent Advocate Requirements

Program must provide access to an independent advocate who:

1. **Independent:** Not a program staff, volunteer, or board member
2. **Trained:** Understands program policies, safeguarding principles, and complainant rights
3. **Available:** Accessible during business hours; emergency contact for safeguarding incidents
4. **Confidential:** Communications with advocate are confidential (not shared with program without consent)
5. **Supportive:** Helps complainant understand process, prepare statements, exercise rights
6. **Accessible:** Provides accommodations (language, disability, cultural) to enable complainant participation

**Advocate role:**
- Explain reporting process and complainant rights
- Help complainant prepare written or oral statements
- Accompany complainant to meetings or interviews
- Review investigation findings for fairness (not legal advice)
- Support complainant through appeal process
- Refer complainant to external resources (legal, mental health, victim services)

## 5. Response Procedures

### 5.1 Feedback Response Procedure

```
Step 1: Coordinator receives feedback (any channel)
Step 2: Coordinator logs feedback in tracking system (case number assigned)
Step 3: Coordinator acknowledges receipt within 5 business days
Step 4: Coordinator reviews feedback and determines action:
        - Implement suggestion (if feasible)
        - Explain why suggestion not adopted (if not feasible)
        - Refer to complaint process (if feedback reveals policy violation)
Step 5: Coordinator responds to complainant within 30 days
Step 6: Coordinator logs response and outcome
Step 7: Safeguarding lead reviews feedback trends quarterly
```

### 5.2 Complaint Response Procedure

```
Step 1: Coordinator or safeguarding lead receives complaint (any channel except anonymous)
Step 2: Safeguarding lead logs complaint in tracking system (case number assigned)
Step 3: Safeguarding lead acknowledges receipt within 3 business days
Step 4: Safeguarding lead reviews complaint and determines:
        - Is this a complaint or safeguarding incident? (If latter, transfer to incident procedure)
        - Is investigator needed? (Usually yes)
        - Who is designated investigator? (Must be independent, trained, available)
Step 5: Safeguarding lead assigns investigator within 5 business days
Step 6: Investigator contacts complainant within 3 business days to:
        - Explain investigation process
        - Obtain complainant's statement
        - Identify witnesses or evidence
        - Discuss accommodations needed
Step 7: Investigator conducts investigation:
        - Interviews complainant, respondent, witnesses
        - Reviews documents, records, physical evidence
        - Maintains confidentiality and identity protection
        - Completes investigation within 30 days (can extend to 60 with approval)
Step 8: Investigator prepares written findings:
        - Summary of allegations
        - Evidence reviewed
        - Findings of fact (what happened)
        - Conclusion (policy violation or not)
        - Recommendations (remedy, training, personnel action, policy change)
Step 9: Safeguarding lead reviews findings and determines:
        - Are findings supported by evidence?
        - Are recommendations appropriate?
        - Is additional investigation needed?
Step 10: Safeguarding lead issues decision to complainant and respondent within 5 business days:
         - Written decision with findings, conclusions, recommendations
         - Notice of appeal rights (complainant can appeal within 15 days)
         - Implementation timeline for recommendations
Step 11: Complainant or respondent can appeal:
         - Appeal to Program Director (or Board if Program Director involved)
         - Appeal must be in writing within 15 days
         - Appeal limited to: new evidence, procedural error, disproportionate remedy
         - Appellate authority reviews and issues final decision within 30 days
Step 12: Safeguarding lead implements recommendations and monitors compliance
Step 13: Safeguarding lead logs outcome in tracking system
Step 14: Safeguarding lead reports complaint trends to board quarterly
```

### 5.3 Safeguarding Incident Response Procedure

```
Step 1: Any person receives incident report (any channel)
Step 2: Person immediately notifies safeguarding lead (or Program Director if safeguarding lead unavailable)
Step 3: Safeguarding lead logs incident in tracking system (case number assigned; marked URGENT)
Step 4: Safeguarding lead acknowledges receipt within 24 hours
Step 5: Safeguarding lead conducts immediate safety assessment within 48 hours:
        - Is complainant safe? (If not, activate crisis response)
        - Are beneficiaries safe? (If not, suspend program contact if needed)
        - Is respondent safe? (If not, provide support)
        - Is immediate medical care needed? (If yes, call emergency services)
        - Is mandatory reporting required? (If yes, contact legal counsel immediately)
Step 6: Safeguarding lead determines response:
        - If mandatory reporting required: Legal counsel makes report within 24 hours; safeguarding lead cooperates with authorities
        - If program suspension needed: Program Director suspends program or removes personnel personnel; safeguarding lead notifies affected parties
        - If investigation needed: Safeguarding lead assigns designated investigator (see 5.2 Step 5)
Step 7: Investigator conducts investigation (see 5.2 Steps 6-8):
        - Investigation completed within 30 days (can extend to 60 with safeguarding lead approval)
        - Investigator provides weekly updates to safeguarding lead
Step 8: Investigator prepares written findings (see 5.2 Step 8)
Step 9: Safeguarding lead reviews findings and determines:
        - Are findings supported by evidence?
        - Are recommendations appropriate?
        - Is additional investigation needed?
        - Is mandatory reporting still required? (Reassess if new information)
Step 10: Safeguarding lead issues decision to complainant and respondent within 5 business days:
         - Written decision with findings, conclusions, recommendations
         - Notice of appeal rights (complainant can appeal within 15 days)
         - Implementation timeline for recommendations
         - Notice of mandatory reporting (if applicable)
Step 11: Complainant or respondent can appeal (see 5.2 Step 11)
Step 12: Safeguarding lead implements recommendations and monitors compliance:
         - Personnel action (termination, suspension, training, supervision)
         - Program change (policy revision, procedure update, training)
         - Beneficiary support (counseling, medical care, legal aid, shelter)
         - Law enforcement cooperation (if criminal investigation ongoing)
Step 13: Safeguarding lead logs outcome in tracking system
Step 14: Safeguarding lead reports incident to board within 7 days
Step 15: Board reviews and determines if additional action needed (public statement, funder notification, regulatory report)
Step 16: Safeguarding lead conducts post-incident review within 30 days:
         - What worked well?
         - What could be improved?
         - Are policy or procedure changes needed?
         - Are training or supervision changes needed?
Step 17: Safeguarding lead implements improvements and monitors
```

### 5.4 Unsafe Disclosure Crisis Response Procedure

```
Step 1: Person receives disclosure (any channel, but usually in-person or phone)
Step 2: Person immediately ensures discloser safety:
        - Is discloser in immediate danger? (If yes, call emergency services)
        - Does discloser need medical care? (If yes, call emergency services)
        - Is discloser a minor or vulnerable adult? (If yes, mandatory reporting likely required)
Step 3: Person notifies safeguarding lead immediately (or Program Director if unavailable)
Step 4: Safeguarding lead activates crisis response team (within 1 hour):
        - Safeguarding lead (coordinates response)
        - Independent advocate (supports discloser)
        - Legal counsel (advises on mandatory reporting and liability)
        - Program Director (authorizes resources and program changes)
Step 5: Crisis response team conducts safety assessment (within 2 hours):
        - Is discloser safe now? (If not, activate emergency shelter or protection)
        - Are other beneficiaries at risk? (If yes, notify and protect)
        - Is mandatory reporting required? (Legal counsel makes determination within 24 hours)
        - Does discloser need immediate support? (Medical, psychological, legal, shelter)
Step 6: Crisis response team provides immediate support:
        - Medical care (if needed and consented)
        - Psychological first aid (trauma-informed, by trained professional)
        - Legal aid (if needed and consented)
        - Emergency shelter (if needed and consented)
        - Independent advocate assigned to discloser
Step 7: Legal counsel makes mandatory reporting decision (within 24 hours):
        - If required: Legal counsel makes report to authorities; safeguarding lead cooperates
        - If not required: Legal counsel documents reasoning; safeguarding lead continues support
Step 8: Safeguarding lead notifies discloser of:
        - Mandatory reporting (if applicable) and what happens next
        - Support services available (advocate, counseling, legal aid, shelter)
        - Confidentiality limits (who will know, why)
        - Discloser's rights (to refuse services, to withdraw consent, to appeal)
Step 9: Safeguarding lead documents disclosure and response:
        - Written record of disclosure (discloser's words, not investigator's interpretation)
        - Safety assessment findings
        - Support services provided
        - Mandatory reporting decision and reasoning
        - Ongoing support plan
Step 10: Safeguarding lead monitors discloser safety and wellbeing:
         - Daily check-in for first week (or more frequent if needed)
         - Weekly check-in for first month
         - Monthly check-in for first year
         - Adjust frequency based on discloser's needs and safety
Step 11: Safeguarding lead conducts post-incident review (within 30 days):
         - What worked well?
         - What could be improved?
         - Are policy or procedure changes needed?
         - Are training or supervision changes needed?
Step 12: Safeguarding lead implements improvements and monitors
```

### 5.5 Retaliation Response Procedure

```
Step 1: Person receives retaliation allegation (any channel)
Step 2: Person immediately notifies safeguarding lead (or Program Director if safeguarding lead involved)
Step 3: Safeguarding lead logs allegation in tracking system (case number assigned; marked RETALIATION)
Step 4: Safeguarding lead acknowledges receipt within 24 hours
Step 5: Safeguarding lead conducts immediate protection assessment within 48 hours:
        - Is complainant safe from further retaliation? (If not, implement immediate protection)
        - Is complainant's program participation at risk? (If yes, suspend adverse action pending investigation)
        - Does complainant need support? (Independent advocate, counseling, legal aid)
Step 6: Safeguarding lead assigns designated investigator (must be independent of alleged retaliation)
Step 7: Investigator conducts investigation (see 5.2 Steps 6-8):
        - Investigation completed within 30 days
        - Investigator provides weekly updates to safeguarding lead
Step 8: Investigator prepares written findings (see 5.2 Step 8)
Step 9: Safeguarding lead reviews findings and issues decision (see 5.2 Steps 9-10)
Step 10: If retaliation substantiated:
         - Personnel action against respondent (termination, suspension, training, supervision)
         - Remedy to complainant (restore position, compensate for losses, apologize)
         - Program change to prevent recurrence (policy, procedure, training, supervision)
Step 11: Safeguarding lead implements recommendations and monitors compliance
Step 12: Safeguarding lead logs outcome in tracking system
Step 13: Safeguarding lead reports retaliation trends to board quarterly
```

## 6. Record Retention and Access

### 6.1 Retention Schedule

| Record Type | Retention Period | Storage Location | Access Control | Disposition |
|-------------|------------------|------------------|----------------|-------------|
| **Feedback records** | 3 years from response date | Program database | Coordinator, safeguarding lead | Destroy after retention |
| **Complaint records** | 7 years from resolution date | Secure database (separate from program records) | Safeguarding lead, investigator, legal counsel | Destroy after retention |
| **Safeguarding incident records** | Permanent (or 10 years after resolution, whichever longer) | Secure database (separate from program records) | Safeguarding lead, investigator, legal counsel, board chair | Archive after 10 years; review for destruction |
| **Unsafe disclosure records** | Permanent (or 10 years after resolution, whichever longer) | Secure database (separate from program records) | Safeguarding lead, crisis response team, legal counsel, board chair | Archive after 10 years; review for destruction |
| **Retaliation records** | 7 years from resolution date | Secure database (separate from program records) | Safeguarding lead, investigator, legal counsel | Destroy after retention |
| **Complainant identity records** | Same as underlying record | Separate from report content (see 3.3) | Safeguarding lead only | Destroy with underlying record |
| **Investigator notes** | Same as underlying record | Secure database | Investigator, safeguarding lead | Destroy with underlying record |
| **Legal counsel advice** | Permanent | Legal department files | Legal counsel, board chair | Archive; never destroy |

### 6.2 Access Control

**Who can access records:**

| Role | Feedback | Complaint | Incident | Disclosure | Retaliation |
|------|----------|-----------|----------|------------|-------------|
| **Complainant** | Own reports only | Own reports only | Own reports only | Own reports only | Own reports only |
| **Respondent** | N/A | Findings and decision (not complainant identity) | Findings and decision (not complainant identity) | N/A (unless respondent) | Findings and decision (not complainant identity) |
| **Coordinator** | All feedback | All feedback | Incident log (case number, status, not details) | Disclosure log (case number, status, not details) | Retaliation log (case number, status, not details) |
| **Investigator** | N/A | Assigned cases only (full access) | Assigned cases only (full access) | N/A | Assigned cases only (full access) |
| **Safeguarding lead** | All feedback (aggregate for trends) | All complaints (full access) | All incidents (full access) | All disclosures (full access) | All retaliation (full access) |
| **Independent advocate** | N/A | Complainant's reports (with consent) | Complainant's reports (with consent) | Discloser's reports (with consent) | Complainant's reports (with consent) |
| **Legal counsel** | N/A | Assigned cases only (full access) | All incidents (full access) | All disclosures (full access) | Assigned cases only (full access) |
| **Program Director** | Aggregate trends | Aggregate trends | Incident log (case number, status, not details) | Incident log (case number, status, not details) | Incident log (case number, status, not details) |
| **Board chair** | Annual aggregate report | Annual aggregate report | Quarterly aggregate report | Quarterly aggregate report | Quarterly aggregate report |
| **Funder/regulator** | Aggregate (no individual data) | Aggregate (no individual data) | Aggregate (no individual data) | Aggregate (no individual data) | Aggregate (no individual data) |

### 6.3 Record Security

**Electronic records:**
- Encrypted at rest (AES-256 or equivalent)
- Encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3 or equivalent)
- Access logged (who accessed what, when)
- Backup daily; tested quarterly
- Stored on secure server (not personal devices or cloud services without encryption)

**Paper records:**
- Stored in locked file cabinet
- Access logged (who accessed what, when)
- Copy electronic (scan and encrypt) then destroy paper after 30 days
- Backup in separate secure location

**Record destruction:**
- Electronic: Secure deletion (not just "delete" but overwrite or cryptographic erasure)
- Paper: Cross-cut shredding
- Destruction logged (what destroyed, when, by whom)
- Retention schedule reviewed annually by safeguarding lead

## 7. Tabletop Exercises

### 7.1 Exercise 1: Anonymous Feedback Reveals Policy Violation

**Scenario:** Anonymous feedback submitted via online form: "The volunteer coordinator always schedules meetings at 7pm. Parents can't attend. This is excluding people."

**Tabletop questions:**
1. How does coordinator log this? (Anonymous feedback; no follow-up possible)
2. How does coordinator respond? (Cannot respond to anonymous submitter; acknowledge in next newsletter that scheduling is under review)
3. Is this a complaint? (No; anonymous, so cannot investigate specific allegation)
4. What if coordinator knows who submitted? (Still treat as anonymous; do not contact person unless they self-identify)
5. What if scheduling change is needed? (Coordinator makes change; documents in feedback log; reports trend to safeguarding lead quarterly)

**Expected response:**
- Coordinator logs feedback (case number assigned)
- Coordinator reviews scheduling and confirms 7pm meetings exclude parents
- Coordinator changes meeting times to rotate between 6pm, 7pm, and 12pm (to accommodate different schedules)
- Coordinator announces change in next newsletter: "We heard your feedback about meeting times. We're rotating times to accommodate different schedules."
- Coordinator logs response and outcome
- Safeguarding lead reviews feedback trends quarterly and notes scheduling as recurring theme

**Lessons learned:**
- Anonymous feedback can reveal systemic issues
- Program can act on anonymous feedback even without investigation
- Transparency about changes builds trust

---

### 7.2 Exercise 2: Complaint Escalates to Safeguarding Incident

**Scenario:** Complainant emails coordinator: "I want to file a complaint. The field volunteer touched my child inappropriately during yesterday's activity. I don't feel safe sending my child back."

**Tabletop questions:**
1. How does coordinator respond? (This is not a complaint; this is a safeguarding incident. Coordinator immediately notifies safeguarding lead)
2. How does safeguarding lead respond? (Acknowledges within 24 hours; conducts safety assessment within 48 hours; assigns investigator)
3. Is mandatory reporting required? (Yes; child abuse allegation triggers mandatory reporting in most jurisdictions. Legal counsel contacted immediately)
4. What happens to volunteer? (Suspended pending investigation; no contact with beneficiaries)
5. What happens to program? (Field activities suspended pending safety assessment; alternative arrangements made for beneficiaries)

**Expected response:**
- Coordinator receives email and immediately notifies safeguarding lead (phone call, not email)
- Safeguarding lead acknowledges receipt to complainant within 24 hours (phone call preferred)
- Safeguarding lead contacts legal counsel within 4 hours
- Legal counsel determines mandatory reporting is required and makes report within 24 hours
- Safeguarding lead conducts safety assessment within 48 hours:
  - Complainant's child is safe (no immediate medical needs)
  - Other beneficiaries may be at risk (saf to be interviewed)
  - Volunteer is safe (no immediate risk)
- Safeguarding lead suspends volunteer and volunteer's access to program
- Safeguarding lead assigns designated investigator (external, to avoid conflict of interest)
- Investigator interviews complainant, child (with trauma-informed approach), other beneficiaries, volunteer
- Investigator reviews evidence (activity logs, witness statements, physical evidence)
- Investigator completes investigation within 30 days
- Safeguarding lead reviews findings and issues decision
- If substantiated: Volunteer terminated; program changes implemented; beneficiaries supported
- If not substantiated: Volunteer reinstated with training; complainant notified of outcome and appeal rights

**Lessons learned:**
- Complaints can reveal safeguarding incidents; coordinator must recognize and escalate
- Mandatory reporting is time-sensitive; legal counsel must be involved immediately
- Safety assessment is first priority; investigation is second
- External investigator avoids conflict of interest

### 7.3 Exercise 3: Retaliation Allegation Against Safeguarding Lead

**Scenario:** Complainant contacts independent advocate: "I filed a complaint last month. Now the safeguarding lead is excluding me from meetings and giving me bad assignments. I think this is retaliation."

**Tabletop questions:**
1. How does independent advocate respond? (Advocate supports complainant; helps complainant file retaliation allegation; notifies Program Director because safeguarding lead is alleged retaliator)
2. How does Program Director respond? (Program Director acknowledges within 24 hours; assigns external investigator because internal safeguarding lead is alleged retaliator)
3. What protection is provided to complainant? (Program Director orders safeguarding lead to cease all contact with complainant pending investigation; complainant's assignments restored)
4. What if safeguarding lead denies retaliation? (Investigator determines facts; if substantiated, personnel action; if not, complainant notified of outcome and appeal rights)
5. What if complainant doesn't trust internal process? (Complainant can contact external authorities (regulator, law enforcement) at any time; program cannot prevent this)

**Expected response:**
- Independent advocate receives complaint and helps complainant file retaliation allegation
- Advocate notifies Program Director (not safeguarding lead, because safeguarding lead is alleged retaliator)
- Program Director acknowledges within 24 hours
- Program Director assigns external investigator (contracted in advance for this scenario)
- Program Director orders safeguarding lead to cease all contact with complainant pending investigation
- Program Director restores complainant's assignments and meeting access
- External investigator interviews complainant, safeguarding lead, witnesses
- External investigator reviews evidence (meeting attendance records, assignment logs, email communications)
- External investigator completes investigation within 30 days
- Program Director reviews findings and issues decision
- If substantiated: Safeguarding lead to be determined)
- If not substantiated: Complainant notified of outcome and appeal rights

**Lessons learned:**
- Retaliation can involve senior staff; program must have external investigator option
- Independent advocate plays critical role in supporting complainant
- Immediate protection is required pending investigation
- Program Director must act independently of safeguarding lead in this scenario

### 7.4 Exercise 4: Unsafe Disclosure by Minor

**Scenario:** During a program activity, a 14-year-old beneficiary tells a volunteer: "My stepdad hits me. I don't want to go home. Can you help me?"

**Tabletop questions:**
1. How does volunteer respond? (Volunteer listens; does not investigate; does not promise confidentiality; immediately notifies safeguarding lead)
2. How does safeguarding lead respond? (Safeguarding lead activates crisis response team; ensures child's immediate safety; contacts legal counsel for mandatory reporting)
3. Is mandatory reporting required? (Yes; child abuse disclosure triggers mandatory reporting in all jurisdictions. Legal counsel makes report within 24 hours)
4. What happens to child? (Child protective services contacted; child may be removed from home; program provides support services)
5. What happens to volunteer? (Volunteer supported; may need debriefing; no fault for receiving disclosure)

**Expected response:**
- Volunteer receives disclosure and immediately notifies safeguarding lead (phone call, not email)
- Safeguarding lead ensures child is safe (if child is in immediate danger, call emergency services)
- Safeguarding lead activates crisis response team (within 1 hour)
- Crisis response team includes: safeguarding lead, independent advocate, legal counsel, Program Director
- Legal counsel determines mandatory reporting is required and makes report to child protective services within 24 hours
- Safeguarding lead contacts child protective services to cooperate with investigation
- Independent advocate assigned to child (and parent/guardian if appropriate)
- Crisis response team provides immediate support to child:
  - Medical care (if needed)
  - Psychological first aid (trauma-informed)
  - Emergency shelter (if child cannot return home)
  - Legal aid (if needed)
- Safeguarding lead documents disclosure and response
- Safeguarding lead monitors child's safety and wellbeing (daily check-in for first week)
- Safeguarding lead supports volunteer (debriefing, counseling if needed)
- Safeguarding lead conducts post-incident review within 30 days

**Lessons learned:**
- Unsafe disclosures require immediate crisis response, not investigation
- Mandatory reporting is required for child abuse; legal counsel must be involved immediately
- Child's safety is first priority; program cooperation with authorities is required
- Volunteer who receives disclosure needs support; no fault for receiving disclosure

### 7.5 Exercise 5: Conflict of Interest in Investigation

**Scenario:** Complainant files safeguarding incident allegation against Program Director. Safeguarding lead is close friends with Program Director.

**Tabletop questions:**
1. How does safeguarding lead respond? (Safeguarding lead recognizes conflict of interest; recuses self; assigns external investigator)
2. Who takes over safeguarding lead role? (Board chair designates alternate safeguarding lead (board member with qualifications) or engages external safeguarding consultant)
3. How is complainant protected? (Complainant notified of conflict and recusal; complainant can request alternate contact)
4. How is Program Director protected? (Program Director placed on administrative leave pending investigation; presumption of innocence)
5. What if external investigator finds no misconduct? (Complainant notified of outcome and appeal rights; Program Director reinstated; safeguarding lead reviews process for improvements)

**Expected response:**
- Safeguarding lead recognizes conflict of interest (close friendship with Program Director)
- Safeguarding lead recuses self and notifies Board chair
- Board chair designates alternate safeguarding lead (board member with safeguarding qualifications) or engages external safeguarding consultant
- Alternate safeguarding lead acknowledges receipt to complainant within 24 hours
- Alternate safeguarding lead assigns external investigator (contracted in advance for this scenario)
- Alternate safeguarding lead places Program Director on administrative leave pending investigation
- External investigator conducts investigation (see 5.3 Steps 6-8)
- External investigator completes investigation within 30 days
- Alternate safeguarding lead reviews findings and issues decision
- If substantiated: Program Director terminated; board notified; regulatory report if required
- If not substantiated: Program Director reinstated; complainant notified of outcome and appeal rights

**Lessons learned:**
- Conflict of interest must be recognized and managed; external investigator required
- Board chair must be prepared to designate alternate safeguarding lead
- Administrative leave protects both complainant and respondent during investigation
- Presumption of innocence applies to respondent; outcome communicated to both parties

## 8. Limitations and Deferred Items

### 8.1 Not Covered in This Baseline

1. **Real legal review:** Mandatory reporting requirements vary by jurisdiction; legal counsel must review for specific program locations.
2. **Real safeguarding lead qualification:** This baseline assumes safeguarding lead exists; actual qualification and training requirements must be defined by program.
3. **Real investigator training:** This baseline assumes investigators are trained; actual training curriculum and certification must be developed.
4. **Real technology infrastructure:** Secure databases, encrypted storage, and access control systems must be built or procured.
5. **Real independent advocate network:** This baseline assumes advocates are available; actual recruitment, training, and deployment must be planned.
6. **Real crisis response team:** This baseline assumes team exists; actual team composition, training, and deployment must be planned.
7. **Real mandatory reporting procedures:** This baseline assumes legal counsel makes reports; actual procedures, forms, and contacts must be established for each jurisdiction.
8. **Real record retention systems:** This baseline assumes secure storage exists; actual systems, backup, and destruction procedures must be implemented.

### 8.2 Requires Owner Action

1. **Legal review:** Legal counsel must review mandatory reporting requirements for each program jurisdiction.
2. **Safeguarding lead recruitment:** Program must recruit and train qualified safeguarding lead.
3. **Investigator training:** Program must train designated investigators (internal or external).
4. **Independent advocate network:** Program must establish network of independent advocates.
5. **Crisis response team:** Program must establish and train crisis response team.
6. **Technology infrastructure:** Program must build or procure secure record-keeping systems.
7. **Mandatory reporting procedures:** Program must establish procedures for each jurisdiction.
8. **Board approval:** Board must approve policies and procedures before program launch.
9. **Funder notification:** Funders must be notified of safeguarding policies and procedures.
10. **Partner consultation:** Local partners must review and approve procedures.
